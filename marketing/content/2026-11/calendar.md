# November posts: Firm Foundation

Seventeen feed posts from Monday 2 November to Tuesday 1 December 2026, four a week, Monday to Thursday, plus Story ideas for the other days and five spares. Built from Josiah's own photos and the website's own drawings and guides; every fact in a caption comes from the website or the source it names. The rules for posting are in `../../posting-rules.md`.

**Who does what.** Will schedules each week on Sunday evening in Meta Business Suite, which posts to Instagram and Facebook together once the Page is linked. Josiah films three short clips (posts 04, 06 and 17), answers comments and messages, and texts the two customers in posts 05 and 14 to ask before their reviews are shared. Each Wednesday post also goes on the Google profile as an update.

**How to post one.** Copy the caption exactly, add the alt text to each image, tag the location "Mount Pleasant, South Carolina", and post at the time listed. The images and videos are in `images/` and `reels/`, named by post number.

**Why these times.** Sprout Social's March 2026 data puts the best Instagram windows at Tuesday and Wednesday afternoons, Monday 2 to 4 PM and Thursday 12 to 2 PM, with weekends lowest ([Sprout Social](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/best-times-to-post-on-instagram/)). Buffer's 2026 data favours weekday mornings and evenings ([Buffer](https://buffer.com/best-time-to-post)). After 30 days, check Instagram's Insights for when the followers are online and move the times to match.

**Why five hashtags.** Instagram began limiting posts to five hashtags in December 2025, rolling it out gradually. The town name in the caption does more for Instagram search than tags.

## 03. Wed 4 Nov, 12:30 PM · Carousel · How it's built

**Media:** `images/03-1.jpg`, `images/03-2.jpg`

**Caption** (copy all of it):

```
Most of a paver patio is under the pavers.

From the top down: pavers with sand swept into the joints, a thin layer of bedding sand, at least 4 inches of compacted stone base, then firm, compacted soil, with fabric between the base and the soil where the ground stays wet. An edge restraint keeps the pavers from creeping, and the surface falls away from the house so water runs off.

When pavers sink or shift, look at the base first. The 4-inch figure is the Concrete Masonry and Hardscapes Association's minimum for patios on well-drained soil.

Thinking about a patio this winter? Text (843) 998-5593.

#mountpleasantsc #charlestonsc #hardscape #paverpatio #flagstone
```

Before you cut down a tree in Mount Pleasant, measure it.

On a residential lot the Town protects trees 16 inches or more across, measured 4 1/2 feet off the ground, and pines from 24 inches. A short list of species, sweet gum among them, never needs a permit.

The request goes through the Town's online permit system and costs $50. Take a protected tree down without one and the Town fines both the homeowner and whoever cut it. That's why we ask about the permit before we schedule any tree work.

The full answer, with the Town's own rules, is on our website under Guides.

## 10. Tue 17 Nov, 1:00 PM · Reel · How it's built

**Media:** `reels/grading-drawing.mp4`

**Caption** (copy all of it):

```
If water sits against your house after it rains, check which way the ground slopes.

Building code asks for the ground to fall at least 6 inches in the first 10 feet away from a house (International Residential Code R401.3). When it slopes toward the house instead, rainwater runs back to the foundation.

Regrading moves soil from the high side to the low side and packs it, so the finished ground falls away from the house.

Not sure which way yours runs? Text us a photo after the next rain: (843) 998-5593.

#mountpleasantsc #charlestonsc #excavation #grading #drainage
```

Swale or French drain? It depends how much fall the yard has.

A swale is a shallow, grassy dip that carries water across the surface. A French drain is a trench lined with fabric and filled with stone around a perforated pipe, for where the ground is too flat to move water on top. Either one has to run to somewhere the water is allowed to go.

Standing water in your yard? Text a photo to (843) 998-5593.
