# Posting rules for Firm Foundation's Instagram and Facebook

For Josiah, posting from his phone. Short on purpose. Written 2026-11-01; the
month of ready posts is in `content/2026-11/`.

## The rhythm

1. **Four posts a week, Monday to Thursday.** Buffer's data shows the biggest
   jump in growth going from one or two posts a week to three to five, and that
   skipping a whole week hurts ([Buffer, 2025](https://buffer.com/resources/how-often-to-post-on-instagram/)).
   If four is too many, drop the Monday post; three a week is still in range.
2. **Will schedules the week on Sunday evening** in Meta Business Suite
   (business.facebook.com), which posts to Instagram and Facebook together once
   the Page is linked and Will is an admin. The November posts are already
   made, so the job site never decides whether a post goes up. Josiah's part is
   small: film the three short clips, answer comments and messages, and take a
   before and an after photo on every job.
3. **Post Monday to Thursday afternoons.** Monday 2:30 PM, Tuesday 1 PM,
   Wednesday and Thursday 12:30 PM, from Sprout Social's March 2026 data
   ([Sprout Social](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/best-times-to-post-on-instagram/)).
   Weekends are the weakest days. After a month, open Insights, look at when
   the followers are online, and move the times to match.
4. **Stories on the other days, when it's easy.** A photo from the job with the
   town on it is enough.
5. **Go past Instagram.** About 200 people follow the account, so the same
   photo and caption also go where neighbors already look: each Wednesday post
   goes on the Google profile as an update (business.google.com, Add update),
   and on the Nextdoor business page once it exists. No hashtags there.

## Every post

6. **The town and the job in the first line.** People search Instagram by
   words now. "Paver patio, Mount Pleasant" helps more than a hashtag.
7. **Five hashtags at most.** Instagram began limiting posts to five in
   December 2025, rolling it out gradually
   ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-implements-new-limits-on-hashtag-use/808309/)).
   Two local ones (#mountpleasantsc, #charlestonsc) and two or three about the job.
8. **Tag the location as the town,** "Mount Pleasant, South Carolina". Never
   the street.
9. **Add alt text** to each photo (Advanced settings, then Accessibility): one
   short sentence saying what's in it. The November posts have it written.
10. **Turn on "Share to Facebook"** once the Page is linked. It remembers.
11. **Music on Reels** comes from Instagram's own library. If a song isn't
    available to the account, pick another.

## Photos and video

12. **Take the before photo before you start,** from a spot you can find again
    (the mailbox, a corner of the house), and the after from the same spot.
    The shot list is in `shot-list.md`.
13. **Phone upright for Reels, sideways for the website.** Instagram shows
    3:4 photos as shot, so photos for Instagram can stay in the camera's normal
    setting ([Mosseri, May 2025, via exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/digital-news/instagram-introduces-34-aspect-ratio-for-photo-uploads-143973.html)).
14. **Only your own photos and video.** Since April 2026 Instagram stops
    recommending accounts that mostly repost other people's content, and a
    credit line doesn't count as original ([Instagram, April 2026](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/rewarding-original-creators-on-instagram)).
    No manufacturer clips, no other contractors' videos, no stock.
15. **The machine photos from the clearing crew are your experience,** not
    Firm Foundation jobs. Caption them "Josiah operating", and don't name the
    crew.

## Customers

16. **Ask before you post someone's house.** A text that says yes is enough.
17. **Keep out of the frame:** house numbers, street signs, license plates,
    and people's faces, especially children.
18. **Reviews:** share only real ones, word for word, with the customer's OK,
    as first name and last initial
    ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)).
    Never offer anything in return for a review, and never ask only the
    customers you expect to be happy: both are against Google's rules
    ([Google](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114)).
    Don't tell anyone what to write or how many stars to give.
19. **Never ask anyone for a Yelp review.** Yelp forbids it and hides reviews
    that were asked for.

## What never goes in a post

19. "Licensed", "insured", "bonded" or "certified". Those are legal claims, and
    the website makes none of them.
20. Prices, until Josiah decides to publish ranges.
21. "Best in Charleston" or any claim nobody can check.

## Comments and messages

20. **Answer every comment and message within a day.** A message is often a
    quote request.
21. **Take price questions to a call or text:** "Happy to look. Can you text a
    photo to (843) 998-5593?"
22. **Never argue in public.** Answer a complaint once, politely, and offer to
    talk by phone.

## Once a month

25. Open Insights and note the three posts with the most saves and shares.
    Text Will what they were. Next month's plan copies what worked.
